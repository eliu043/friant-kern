# The ground beneath the canal

The Friant–Kern Canal carries water by gravity over a 152-mile route. This story connects its regional terrain, dated ground movement, and documented capacity correction work. City populations are optional context, not a chapter in the argument.

## Reading the layers

USGS terrain supplies a regional 3D view and a sampled west–east transect at 36.2° N. The transect intersects the reference alignment near Lindsay; it is not an along-canal hydraulic profile. The exported raster has approximately 200 m ground spacing at the section latitude. Acquisition dates vary; the service snapshot is October 2026 and retrieval was November 2026. The 3D height exaggeration is 2×; the profile exaggeration is approximately 23×. The bounded model extent is outlined; outside it is basemap context.

- Canal: a historical reference centerline, not an as-built engineering survey or service-area boundary.
- Communities: 28 incorporated cities within a regional map window, with July 2025 Census population estimates at 2020 Gazetteer reference points. Circle area is proportional to population. This excludes unincorporated places and rural residents, and does not measure canal customers or exposure.
- Ground: DWR / TRE ALTAMIRA cumulative vertical displacement, 13 June 2015–1 January 2024. These regional estimates are interpolated and resampled; they do not measure canal slope or flow. Blank cells have no data.
- Samples: three illustrative raster cells near Lindsay (−0.02 ft), Terra Bella (−2.22 ft), and Delano (−0.12 ft). These are not representative town statistics or canal surveys.

## The engineering story

Friant Water Authority reports more than 60% historical capacity loss in the critical 10-mile section targeted in Phase 1. Reclamation celebrated completion of the first phase in June 2024 within a broader approximately 33-mile correction effort. The satellite interval ends before that completion and cannot assess repair performance.

Reclamation's 2023 construction account describes provision for about 5 ft of future subsidence in project design. This is a design allowance, not a forecast. USGS explains that compaction of fine-grained aquifer sediments at historically low groundwater levels can be irreversible. The prototype does not quantify storage loss or attribute ground movement to particular wells.
