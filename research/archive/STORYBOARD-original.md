# Five-chapter storyboard

## Chosen: The ground beneath the canal

Reader question: How can a fixed water-conveyance line become vulnerable to movement in the ground?

| Chapter | New evidence / map treatment | Reader action | Defensible takeaway | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The line | Full canal reference alignment, regional context, north-up overview | Scroll; optionally explore/reset | Infrastructure has a geographic continuity | No service-area shading; alignment may predate repairs |
| 2. The ground | Add 2015–Jan2024 cumulative displacement beneath the same line; modest zoom | Compare darker/lighter areas; adjust opacity or context | Regional movement is spatially uneven | Fixed bins, dated units, transparent nodata; no simulated flow |
| 3. The measurement | Add three actual raster-cell sample locators; retain both prior layers | Click dots to read sampled values | Regional estimates differ; sampling scale matters | Not survey of lining, slope or whole town; no precision claim from decimals |
| 4. The consequence | Retain measured context; frame the southern middle-reach landscape | Read engineering citation; explore map | Agency records document capacity consequences and repair | Do not invent project boundary; 33-mile scope differs from 10-mile first phase |
| 5. The limit | Return to wider frame, retain layers and uncertainty | Open evidence desk, rejected-site audit, downloads | Mechanism and documented impacts support a bounded argument | No individual-well attribution; no post-Jan2024 measurement; no prediction |

Every scene sets a complete layer state, so reverse scrolling and direct chapter jumps work. North-up camera only, 650 ms ease, no tilt/rotation. Reduced-motion preference sets duration to zero. The map does not capture wheel scrolling. On mobile the map occupies the upper 47% of the viewport, with text beneath; explicit chapter navigation and source links supplement scrolling. The reader can drag/zoom after selecting Explore, toggle context, change evidence opacity, reset, and inspect sample points.

## Rejected conditional North Crimean storyboard

This sequence is preserved for future development **only after the service-area and comparison gaps close**. It is not implemented as if the missing evidence existed.

| Chapter | Intended revelation | Required evidence / current status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A landscape that looks continuous | Crop/land-use base layer, mainland and Crimea distinguished | A dated agricultural footprint; not inferred from green imagery alone |
| 2. An engineered territory | NCC alignment, branches, actual command areas; distinguish Kakhovka Main Canal | Verified historical service polygons missing; no buffer substitute |
| 3. A baseline already interrupted | 2014 interruption / 2021 irrigation conditions | 2021 raster covers two mainland oblasts, not full Crimea; cannot treat 2021 Crimea as intact canal baseline |
| 4. Two dates, multiple disruptions | Common-mask 2021/2024 classification overlay, chronology of 2022 and June2023 | Grid compatibility verified; semantic, classifier and territory compatibility unresolved |
| 5. What loss can mean | Observed classification change vs competing explanations | Need field/administrative validation, crop/weather controls and delivery records; sole-cause claim rejected |

A successful future NCC project could present an evidence-gap atlas or focus explicitly on the mainland multi-canal region. Neither would be the requested canal-specific loss comparison without additional evidence. This prototype follows the requested fallback instead.
